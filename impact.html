01The ground

When the market moves, a whole economy loses its bearings.

The livestock trade rests on a place and on familiar faces. In Bamako, that place has moved. In Burkina Faso and Niger, it is the roads and access to points of sale that have become harder. Everywhere, those who live from livestock pay the price. We are building for what is happening now, not for a theoretical problem.

September 2024

Bamako's livestock markets close

By decision of the district governor, for reasons of public order, seven markets in the capital close and replacement sites are announced outside the city.1 A decision whose reasons everyone understands: the market could no longer work as it did.

What we observe

Selling has moved away from the city

Today, the capital's livestock trade takes place on sites outside the city. Its effects on those who lived from it are very real.

What we are told

Sellers, buyers, trades: all affected

The sellers we talk to tell us they have lost the place where buyers knew them, and their income with it. Buyers no longer know where to find them. The butchers and transporters who worked around the markets have lost customers too. What they ask for: a market open at any hour, reachable from anywhere, including for the family buying from abroad.Words of Bamako sellers collected by Garabaly, reported without names.
The country's strength

Strong, steady demand

Demand for livestock is driven by consumption in the cities and by exports to coastal countries, and prices are holding above the seasonal average.13

What makes selling harder

In some areas, delays and costs

In some areas, bringing in animals and goods takes longer and costs more, and herders have less easy access to pasture and water points.13

How Garabaly can help

In support of the country's priorities

The National Development Plan 2026-2030 makes livestock markets and pastoral areas a priority.16 Garabaly comes in support: a market that does not depend on a place, where the seller is visible at any hour and the buyer pays in a protected way.

The country's strength

Pastoral herding at the heart of the economy

In pastoral areas, households live from selling their animals, and demand from coastal countries keeps prices average to above average.14

What makes selling harder

In some areas, a point of sale that is harder to reach

In some areas, reaching a point of sale is harder, and herders sell more animals for a falling income.14

How Garabaly can help

In support of resilience

The Programme de résilience pour la sauvegarde de la patrie (Resilience Programme for Safeguarding the Homeland) puts resilience at the centre.17 Garabaly comes in support: a verified listing reaches buyers beyond the herder's area, in other cities and in the diaspora.

2 in 3

recorded livestock movements in West Africa cross an international border.3 Buyers are often far away: what Bamako is living through, the whole region lives through.

≈ $1B

The herder sells for less. The family pays more for meat.

The demand is there, in the cities as in the coastal countries. But where the security context disrupts roads and markets, both ends of the chain lose out, and the gap between them widens.

02The people

Six trades, six difficulties. One marketplace.

Everyone in the chain takes a risk today. It is not a lack of skill: it is a market that gives them no visibility, no protection and no record.

01 / 06The herder

The herderSeller

TodayHe drives his animals to market without knowing who will buy, or at what price. With no buyer, he sometimes sells at a discount or hands the animal to a middleman. And he goes home with cash in his pocket and no record of the sale.
With GarabalyHe lists his animals: an AI assistant recognises the animal in his photos and fills in a good part of the listing, and he describes the rest by voice, in his own language. Buyers reach him from the city and from abroad. Paid in the app, the money is protected, then paid out with no trip carrying cash. If he is not at ease with writing, a field agent helps him create his account and post his first listing, and a young person around him can then help him along.

The city buyerHouseholds, restaurant owners

TodayHe pays someone he knows in advance, and sometimes receives the wrong animal, the wrong weight, or nothing at all. The money is gone.
With GarabalyHe sees the animal, the seller and the price before committing. His payment stays protected until he confirms he has received the right animal.

The diasporaBuyer abroad

TodayHe sends money, and hope: several middlemen, no proof. Often he only learns afterwards how things went.
With GarabalyHe chooses the animal himself from abroad and pays by card. The money stays protected until delivery to the family is confirmed. Distance no longer decides trust.

The butcherIndependent provider

TodayBefore dawn, he must already know which animal he will work on. He advances money to his contacts and hopes the quality will follow; if not, the loss is his.
With GarabalyHe sees the animal, the seller and the price before buying. Booked as a provider in a sale, he is paid once the service is done.

The vetIndependent provider

TodayHer expertise travels by word of mouth: it is hard to be found, booked and paid by clients who do not know her yet.
With GarabalyA verified, bookable profile, and a protected payment paid out at the end of the service, including for checks ordered from abroad.

The transporterIndependent provider

TodayOne-off jobs, uncertain pay, no way to prove his reliability to a new client: he competes on price, not on his reputation.
With GarabalyBookable in the same sale as the animal, paid by the platform on delivery. Every successful job adds to his track record.

03A sale, from start to finish

Two journeys move side by side: the animal, and the money.

The animal goes from the seller to the buyer. The money waits, protected, until the buyer confirms arrival. No one has to take the other's word for it. Here is a sale, screen by screen, in the app as it is today.

The animal For sale
The money —
1

The buyer chooses the animal

Photos, price, seller, place: everything is visible before committing. The seller prepared the listing with the AI assistant, alone or with a field agent's help, and described the animal by voice.

2

They agree on the price

As at the market, people negotiate. The seller offers a new price; the buyer accepts or refuses.

3

He adds the services he needs

A vet check, a delivery: independent, verified providers, in the same protected sale. The seller always prepares first. Each provider booked is paid once the service is done.

4

He pays, and the money is protected

Mobile money, card or Garabaly wallet: the funds stay protected until confirmation. Paying on delivery is still possible.

5

He confirms, and the seller is paid

Every step is tracked. When the right animal arrives, the buyer confirms with their code: the money is paid to the seller, and the sale leaves a record.

What becomes possible next

These are not products yet. They are the possibilities opened by a market where every sale leaves a record.

PossibilityTraceability and proof of ownership

Verified sellers and a record of every sale can support animal traceability and proof of ownership, working with the livestock services.

PossibilityA price reference

Over time, displayed and agreed prices can serve as a reference for the livestock market, for herders and buyers alike.

PossibilityKnowing the herds traded

The species and breeds traded most, region by region, and, with vets and the livestock services, the most frequent health problems.

PossibilityA trading record that follows the seller

Completed sales and reviews received form a record that could, tomorrow, help a seller access financial services.

PossibilityAn early signal

Unusual sales or price changes, aggregated and anonymous, could feed food-security early-warning systems.

PossibilityMonitoring the sector

Aggregated, anonymous data can support the monitoring of the livestock sector by the institutions responsible for it.
